Fair questions

Before you ask

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You need to read English and paste prompts. Every concept is explained in plain language the moment it matters — and understanding why the pieces fit is the actual curriculum. Typing was never the valuable part.

What does it cost beyond the $30?

A Claude Pro subscription (which powers Claude Code) and a free-tier AWS account. Expected AWS spend for the whole course: $0–2. Our own complete run billed $0.00 — and chapter 0's first real act is arming a $5 budget alarm so a surprise bill is structurally impossible to miss.

What if something breaks?

One rule, every chapter: paste the error into Claude Code and say "fix this." That's not a cop-out — debugging by directing is the course. And because every chapter was dry-run for real, the troubleshooting sections already cover the failures we hit ourselves, including the weird ones.

What do I actually have when I'm done?

Five deployed-and-documented repos on your GitHub, working knowledge of the eight most-used AWS services, a verified-clean cloud account, and a workflow — direct, checkpoint, debug — that transfers to every provider and every future tool.

I'm already an engineer — is this too basic for me?

The AWS pieces may be familiar. The method is the product: specifying work in plain English, letting Claude execute, verifying with checkpoints, debugging by directing. It's not about the services — it's realizing how much of your day can run this way. You'll finish with the workflow, plus five clean reference implementations of it.

Why is there a teardown chapter? I paid to build things!

Because knowing how to leave a cloud account is what separates people who've done tutorials from people who've run infrastructure. Your GitHub keeps the proof forever; your AWS account returns to zero; and everything rebuilds from the chapters in about an hour whenever you want it back.
